# Critique — Treasury ALM & Risk Blueprint and D2 Core

Adversarial read of `treasury-alm-risk-platform` and `d2-instrument-position-core` against
`Tier1-Bank-Treasury-Instrument-Universe-and-Balance-Sheet-Taxonomy (1).docx`, banking and
regulatory reality, and internal consistency. Scope decisions in the blueprint's scope table are
treated as settled; the critique tests the design *given* them.

**Verdict.** The spine is sound — Contract/Leg/Cashflow/Position/Valuation is the right shape, the
bitemporal event model is right, D8's narrow contract is right, and the contractual-and-behavioural
parallelism is right and is the thing most in-house builds get wrong. What does not hold is the
**universality claims** built on top of that spine. Three of them are false as written and one is
load-bearing for the phase plan:

1. Every Part 1 instrument reduces to Leg + Optionality — **false**, two structural concepts are missing.
2. Every Part 2 line is a Position query over the eight dimensions — **false**, roughly half of Part 2 is not a contract and four required distinctions are not among the eight.
3. Phase 0 delivers a complete, classified balance sheet — **false**, the rule engines for two of the eight dimensions are owned by modules built in Phases 4 and 6.

These are fixable without redesigning the core. The fixes are additive: one more rate treatment, one
more object, one more dimension group, and a re-cut of the phase boundaries. But they must be fixed
before a vendor prices against this document, because each one currently reads as "configuration"
and would be discovered as "project."

## 1. Critical — would break the build

### C1. Roughly half of Part 2 is not a Contract, and acceptance criterion 2 demands it be one

D2 §8 criterion 2 requires every line A.1–A.16, B.1–B.14, C.1–C.6, D.1–D.4 (40 line items) to
"generate as a Position query with no bespoke rule." A Position is derived from Contracts (§5). But
these Part 2 lines have no contract, no leg, no schedule and no cashflow:

| Part 2 | Line | Why it is not a Contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A.1 | Vault cash, mandatory reserve balances | Balances, not deals. Mandatory reserves are also effectively encumbered and not freely HQLA |
| A.2 | Nostro balances | Derived from settlement activity (D5), not booked |
| A.6 | *Less: ECL allowance (Stage 1/2/3)* | A valuation adjustment sourced externally — see C8 |
| A.9–A.16 | Associates/JV/subs, PP&E, investment property, ROU assets, goodwill & intangibles, DTA, other assets, held-for-sale | Non-financial and non-contractual |
| B.9–B.11, B.13, B.14 | Provisions, current tax, DTL, other liabilities, held-for-sale | Non-contractual |
| C.1–C.6 | All equity | Non-contractual |

That is ~19 of 40 line items. This is not academic: leverage ratio exposure, NSFR RSF (PP&E carries
100% RSF), CET1 (goodwill and intangibles are deductions, DTA is partly deducted), and EVE all
require them. The blueprint currently has no object that can hold them, and D2 §2.1's mandatory
attributes (trade date, maturity, notional, counterparty, direction) cannot be populated for
goodwill or share capital.

Related: **equity securities are in Part 2 (A.3 trading equities, A.4 FVOCI/strategic equity) but**
**not in Part 1's instrument universe**, and D2 §2.2 has no worked decomposition for a cash equity
position. An equity share has no maturity, no repricing basis, and no contractual cashflow — so
classification dimensions 1, 2 and 3 are undefined for it. D2 §2.4 hard rule 1 ("no Contract stored
without complete classification") would then **block booking an equity holding**. Same problem for
physical commodity holdings (Part 1 §7 names gold).

**Fix.** Introduce a sixth primitive: a **Balance** object — an ingested or GL-sourced carrying
amount with the eight dimensions attached but no legs and no projection. Non-contractual lines,
nostro, reserves, cash and equity/commodity cash holdings become Balances. Position aggregates over
Contracts *and* Balances. Then make "not applicable" a first-class, explicitly-permitted value on
the maturity, behavioural and repricing dimensions, so hard rule 1 does not fire on positions that
legitimately have none. Restate criterion 2 as "every Part 2 line generates as a query over
Positions, where Positions aggregate Contracts and Balances."

### C2. The eight dimensions do not span Part 2 — the central design test fails on its own terms

Appendix B states the design test: can every Part 2 line be generated by aggregating Positions along
product/GL mapping filtered by accounting classification, with no bespoke rule? Run it and it fails
in four places, each needing a dimension that is not among the eight:

| Part 2 requirement | Missing dimension |
| --- | --- |
| A.8 / B.8 — hedge-accounting-designated derivatives, kept separate from trading derivatives | **Hedge designation status.** Currently modelled as a `CONTRACT_LINK` (D2 §2.5), not as a classification dimension, so it cannot be a filter on a Position query |
| A.3 vs A.4, A.3 vs A.7 — trading intent vs banking book | **Trading/banking book boundary.** Implied by `book` (a D1 attribute), but not a classification dimension — and it is a regulatory boundary under FRTB with its own re-designation controls, not a free-text book name |
| A.3 / B.4 — derivatives broken out by risk type: FX, IR, credit, equity, commodity | **Primary risk type.** Not derivable from product code alone: a cross-currency swap is both FX and IR |
| A.6 — ECL allowance by Stage 1/2/3; A.15/A.16/B.14 — held-for-sale | **ECL stage** and **held-for-sale / disposal-group status** |

A.3 and B.4 additionally require splitting the *same* derivative population by sign of fair value at
reporting date, presented gross unless IAS 32 offsetting criteria are met — so those lines depend on
D8 valuation and on netting-agreement enforceability (see C9), not on Position aggregation alone.

**Fix.** Promote hedge designation, book/regulatory-book classification, primary risk type,
ECL stage and held-for-sale status into the classification set. Call it "the eight core dimensions
plus five presentation dimensions" rather than silently widening "eight" — the count is quoted in
both artifacts and will be quoted back. Re-run the Appendix B test line by line and publish the
resulting 40-row map as an appendix; it is the single highest-value validation artefact available
before a build starts, and it is cheap.

### C3. Phase 0 cannot deliver a classified balance sheet — the rule owners arrive in Phases 4 and 6

D2 §2.4 assigns rule ownership: **accounting classification → D7**, **regulatory classification →**
**D13**. The parent's build sequence delivers D7 in Phase 4 and D13 in Phase 6. But hard rule 1 says
no Contract is stored without complete classification, and Phase 0's stated usable output is "a
complete, **classified** balance sheet."

The dependency is not theoretical. Phase 1 (LCR, NSFR, HQLA) consumes exactly the regulatory
classification dimension — HQLA level, run-off factor, ASF/RSF — whose rule owner is five phases
away. Phase 1 is therefore either blocked or is silently building D13's rules engine under a
different name, which is the drift that produces two divergent regulatory rulebooks.

The same shape recurs elsewhere: D2 §5.1's near-real-time decision is justified by **pre-deal limit**
**checks** — which live in D4 (Phase 4) and consume the limit framework inside D11 (Phase 5). And
D15 (Phase 7) owns the four-eyes and audit controls that D2 §2.4 rule 2 mandates from Phase 0, and
owns the regeneration test that C5's retention strategy depends on.

**Fix.** Split the classification rules engine out as its own foundation capability delivered in
Phase 0, with D7 and D13 as the *rule authors* that populate it later rather than as the *systems*
that must exist first. Move the audit trail, four-eyes and override machinery from D15 into Phase 0
and leave D15 as model governance and validation only. Move the limit framework out of D11 into a
cross-cutting capability delivered alongside D4, since it is consumed by D4, D6, D9 and D10, not
just by market risk. Then re-state each phase's usable output honestly: Phase 0 delivers a complete
**contract and balance inventory** with contractual cashflows, not a classified balance sheet.

### C4. Leg + Optionality is not enough — the failure mode D2 designed against has already occurred

D2 §2.2 names the risk precisely: "a product that needs a *third* structural concept beyond Leg and
Optionality." It has already happened, twice, in instruments the source document explicitly lists:

**(a) Total-return legs.** Leg rate treatment is "fixed **or** floating (index, tenor, spread,
multiplier, reset, compounding)". A **total return swap** (Part 1 §5 and §6) and an **equity swap**
(Part 1 §7) have a leg whose cashflow is *price appreciation plus distributions on a reference*
*asset*. That is neither fixed nor floating-index. Booking it as linked Contracts (the §2.2 escape
hatch) does not help — the return leg is still one leg with no expressible rate treatment.

**(b) Externally-projected pool cashflows.** An **ABS/MBS holding** (Part 1 §3) is not "1 leg:
coupon schedule + redemption." Its cashflows are a function of pool factor, CPR/CDR, credit
enhancement and a **tranche waterfall**. You do not own the underlying loans, so D2's
contract-level-projection premise (§4.4) cannot apply, and a waterfall cannot be expressed as legs.
In practice these cashflows come from an external model or provider. The same shape applies to
**index CDS** (a 125-name pool with a factor that steps on defaults) and **synthetic securitisation**
(tranched protection with attachment/detachment points, not legs).

**(c) Commodity legs.** Leg has "exactly one currency." A commodity leg is quantity × price in
units (ounces, barrels). Gold is workable by treating XAU as a currency; energy is not.

**(d) Futures.** Appendix A says futures "need margin handling via D6", but the deeper issue is that
a future generates **no contractual cashflows at all** — only daily variation margin, which is
unknowable in advance. It still belongs in the repricing gap.

**Fix.** Add a third rate treatment — `return` — carrying reference asset, price source, reset
convention and distribution pass-through, and add a `quantity` economics variant for commodity legs
(with a documented XAU-as-currency convention for precious metals). Add an amortisation type
`externally-projected`, where D2 stores and versions a supplied cashflow vector rather than
generating it, with an explicit provenance record. Then update §2.2's worked decompositions to
include TRS, equity swap, ABS/MBS, index CDS, collateral swap and a bond future, and re-test. Note
that `externally-projected` is a **stored, not regenerable** input — it must move to §7.1 (see C5).

### C5. The projection function is not deterministic as specified, and the retention strategy rests on that determinism

§7.4 correctly identifies the dependency: if projection is not exactly reproducible across code
versions, retention "fails silently and undetectably." Three concrete reasons it is not reproducible
as currently written:

1. **The signature is missing an input.** `project(Contract, as_of_date, basis, assumption_set, horizon)`
 — but §4.1 step 3 says future resets come from D3's forward curve and inflation notionals index
 off D3. So the output depends on a market data snapshot that is not a parameter. Same function
 call, different day, different answer. (This also means the parent's data-flow diagram is wrong:
 it routes market data only into Valuation, not into Cashflow projection.)
2. **D1 reference data is not in the stored/versioned list.** §7.1 versions market data, model
 parameters, scenarios and classification rules — but **not holiday calendars, day-count and roll**
** conventions, product catalogue, GL map, counterparty static or index definitions**. Holiday
 calendars are mutable and get retroactively corrected; a single added public holiday shifts
 payment dates and accruals on historic projections. Benchmark reform (an index that no longer
 exists, with contractual fallbacks) is the decade-scale version of the same problem.
3. **Floating point over a decade.** Bit-exact equality across platform migrations, math library
 versions, FMA contraction and — critically — **summation order, which changes whenever the**
** parallel-by-contract partitioning changes (§4.4)**. Criterion 4 says "reproduces exactly"; §7.4
 says "assert equality." Neither is achievable without a tolerance policy, and once you accept
 tolerance you can no longer distinguish drift from regression.

The proposed mitigation is also thinner than it looks: the periodic regeneration test compares
against **as-reported figures**, which are aggregates — a compensating pair of contract-level errors
passes. It is owned by D15, built in **Phase 7**, so the safety net arrives after years of
projections have been discarded. And there is no stated remediation: if Q3-2029 cannot be
reproduced in 2034, there is no fallback, because the projections were deleted by design.

**Fix.** Four changes, all cheap:

- Add `market_snapshot_id` and `reference_data_version` to the projection signature, and make D1
reference data effective-dated and bitemporal on the same footing as classification rules.
- Store a **deterministic digest per EOD projection run** — a hash per contract plus per-bucket
subtotals. Negligible volume, and it converts "silently undetectable" into "detected next day, at
contract granularity."
- **Freeze and archive full contract-level cashflow detail for regulatory reporting dates only**
(quarter-ends, ~4–20 dates a year). At ~100m rows a date this is entirely affordable and removes
regeneration from the critical path for exactly the dates a regulator will ask about. Regenerate
for everything else.
- Retain the **engine build itself** as a versioned artefact alongside the inputs, and state a
numerical tolerance policy with an escalation threshold rather than asserting exact equality.
- Pull the regeneration test forward to Phase 1. It is a test harness, not a governance module.

### C6. LCR is not a contractual cashflow computation, and Phase 1 needs D6

The Phase 1 claim — liquidity works on contractual cashflows before behavioural models exist — is
**directionally right for the wrong reason**, and the wrong reason hides a real dependency.

Right: LCR and NSFR run-off, inflow, ASF and RSF factors are **regulator-prescribed**, not
bank-calibrated. So Phase 1 genuinely does not need D9's behavioural models. Good call.

Wrong: LCR is a **balance × prescribed factor** computation, not a cashflow-ladder aggregation. A
retail current account contributes 5% or 10% of its *balance* as a 30-day outflow regardless of its
contractual overnight cashflow. The parent's rationale ("LCR and the cashflow ladder come off
contractual cashflows") mis-states the mechanism, and that matters because it makes LCR look like a
free by-product of D2 when it is a separate rules engine (whose owner, per C3, is D13 in Phase 6).

The dependencies this hides:

| LCR/NSFR requirement | Depends on | Phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unencumbered status of HQLA; NSFR encumbrance-duration RSF | D6 collateral & encumbrance | **4** |
| Derivative collateral outflow under the historical look-back approach — largest 30-day net collateral flow over the **preceding 24 months** | Collateral flow history that does not exist at go-live | — |
| 3-notch downgrade trigger outflow | CSA contractual terms (see C9) | 4 |
| Level 2 / 2B caps and the adjusted-HQLA unwind of short-term secured funding | Repo detail from D6 | 4 |
| **Survival horizon** — explicitly listed in Phase 1 | Stressed run-off assumptions, i.e. behavioural | **3** |

**Fix.** Keep liquidity first — the sequencing judgment is right. But re-scope Phase 1 to "LCR, NSFR
and the contractual cashflow ladder on a static encumbrance snapshot," pull a minimal encumbrance
register forward from D6 into Phase 0/1 (it is a small state table, not the whole collateral module),
move survival horizon to Phase 3, and state the HLBA history gap explicitly with its interim
treatment (conservative proxy until 24 months of history accrues). Correct the rationale sentence in
parent §6 — a regulator reading "LCR comes off contractual cashflows" will ask follow-up questions.

### C7. D7 cannot do its job without reading the terms payload, which the design rule forbids

D2 §2.1: "Nothing outside D2 and D8 reads the terms payload." D2 §2.4 assigns **accounting**
**classification (business model + SPPI) to D7**. D2 §6 publishes to D7 only "contract events,
accruals, amortisation schedules."

The SPPI test is a legal-terms analysis of the instrument's contractual cashflow characteristics.
So is the embedded-derivative closely-related assessment (D2 §2.6). So is the effective interest
rate calculation, which needs fees, discount, premium and expected life. None can be performed from
events and schedules. D7 must read terms.

**Fix.** Either widen the rule to "nothing outside D2, D8 and D7 reads the terms payload," or —
better — have D2 publish a **derived accounting-characteristics view**: a structured, stable summary
(cashflow linearity, leverage, contingency, non-recourse features, prepayment terms, embedded
feature inventory, fee and discount components) that D7 consumes. The second keeps the
instrument-agnostic property that makes the design good. Either way it must be stated; leaving it
implicit produces a build where D7 quietly parses product-family payloads and the abstraction dies.

### C8. The ECL boundary is defensible but is currently drawn as a hole, not a boundary

"The platform consumes ECL, it does not compute it" is a reasonable boundary — ECL modelling has its
own model-risk discipline. But nothing specifies the *interface*, and four things break without it:

- **A.6** requires the ECL allowance line by stage. **B.9** requires off-balance-sheet ECL on undrawn
commitments and guarantees. Both are acceptance-criterion-2 lines.
- **Amortised cost carrying amount = gross carrying amount − loss allowance.** Without the allowance,
D2 cannot produce the balance sheet at all, and Part 2 is the module's acceptance test.
- **Stage 3 interest revenue is calculated on the net carrying amount**, so D2's accrual output — which
§6 publishes to D7 — is a function of the allowance. The boundary is not clean at the accrual level.
- ECL's own inputs flow the other way: **EAD for off-balance-sheet items depends on D2's drawdown**
**model** (§4.3), and SICR staging depends on origination-date credit quality held on the Contract.

D2 §6's inbound interface table has **no ECL row at all**.

**Fix.** Add an inbound interface: per-contract ECL stage, loss allowance, PD/LGD/EAD used, model
version and effective date, at contract granularity with an as-of date, subject to the same
reconciliation gate as every other feed. Add ECL stage to the classification set (C2). State
explicitly that D2 **supplies** EAD inputs and drawdown model output to the ECL engine, so the
dependency is two-way and visible. And name the owner of the Stage 3 interest interaction — it is a
D2/D7 joint rule, not an external one.

### C9. There is no Legal Agreement / netting set object anywhere

ISDA master agreements, CSAs, GMRAs and GMSLAs are absent from D1, D2 and D6. The netting set is a
first-class object in Basel counterparty credit risk and has nowhere to live. What breaks:

- **SA-CCR** (D11, D13) computes EAD **per netting set**, not per trade.
- **CVA/DVA** is a netting-set-level calculation.
- **A.3 / B.4** gross vs net derivative presentation depends on IAS 32 offsetting, which depends on
enforceability of the master agreement.
- **LCR downgrade-trigger outflows** (C6) require reading CSA rating triggers.
- **Collateral** (D6) needs CSA threshold, MTA, minimum transfer amount, eligible collateral schedule,
rounding, independent amount and rehypothecation rights.
- Repo/sec-lending recognition depends on GMRA/GMSLA terms.

**Fix.** Add `LEGAL_AGREEMENT` as a foundation entity in D1 (counterparty-level, with type, governing
law, enforceability opinion status, effective and termination dates) and `NETTING_SET` as a grouping
Contracts belong to, referenced from D2's Contract. This is small and it is genuinely blocking for
Phases 4–6.

### C10. Two missing domains, both in the operational and data-management space

The instinct is right. Fifteen domains and neither of these has an owner:

**(a) Data ingestion, reconciliation and data quality.** Parent §4 describes reconciliation in
detail and makes it a **hard gate on the EOD pipeline** — but no domain owns the feed adapters, the
break register, break classification and ageing, the escalation workflow, or the data quality checks
that §5 lists as an NFR. In a real bank this is a subsystem with a team. Right now it is a section
in a document with no D-number, no phase and no build/buy posture, which means it will be built four
times, once per consuming module.

**(b) Batch orchestration and operational control.** Parent §3 specifies a gated EOD pipeline with
stage gating, re-run headroom, and a provisional flag that propagates to downstream reports. Nobody
owns the scheduler, the gate state machine, the dependency graph, the re-run and partial-re-run
semantics, or the propagation of "provisional." "Every stage is gated" is called a non-negotiable and
has no implementer.

Two lesser candidates worth a line each: **corporate actions processing** on held securities
(announcement → entitlement → capture; the event list has `CALLED` and `DEFAULTED` but no ops
process), and **withholding tax** on cross-border coupon receipts, which changes gross to net
cashflow and therefore the liquidity ladder and the accrual.

**Fix.** Add D16 (Data Integration, Reconciliation & Quality) and D17 (Process Orchestration &
Operational Control) to the domain map, in a new operational layer that cuts across rather than
sitting above L1–L5. Give both a Phase 0/1 delivery — they are prerequisites, not conveniences — and
a "buy" posture for the orchestration engine.

## 3. Gaps and ambiguities

### 3.1 Instrument coverage — Part 1 items the model does not yet handle

Beyond C4. These are the ones that were least tested, and most of them are real.

| Instrument | Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Repo / reverse repo / sec lending — recognition** | Neither artifact states the **recognition and derecognition rule**, and it is foundational. Securities lent or repo'd out are **not derecognised** (risks and rewards retained) — they stay as your Position, encumbered. Securities *received* in a reverse repo are **not recognised** as holdings — but they *do* count toward HQLA if eligible and rehypothecable. The Leg model calls the collateral leg "non-cashflow-generating" and says nothing about whether it creates a Position. Get this wrong in either direction and you either double-count HQLA or miss it entirely | State the rule explicitly on the collateral leg: `creates_position` vs `memo_only`, plus `encumbers` and `rehypothecable` flags, and derive HQLA from the combination rather than from the security alone |
| **Tri-party repo** | The collateral leg is a **basket managed by the tri-party agent and reallocated daily**, not a set of known ISINs. Encumbrance is determined by the agent's allocation, arriving as a daily report | Collateral leg needs a `basket` variant: eligibility schedule + concentration limits + as-of composition sourced from an agent feed. Add the tri-party agent as an inbound source in D2 §6 and a reconciliation feed in parent §4 |
| **Collateral swaps** | Securities-for-securities with **no cash leg**. The §2.2 framing "Repo = cash leg + collateral leg" implies cash is always present. Economics are a fee plus two encumbrance changes | Add as a worked decomposition: two collateral legs + one fee leg. It also has no Part 2 home — it is off-balance-sheet with an encumbrance memo |
| **Bankers' acceptances** | A BA is two-sided: accepting creates a **contingent liability (acceptance outstanding)** and a matching **customer receivable**, classically shown gross both sides; discounting your own acceptance nets them. Neither the decomposition nor Part 2 has a place for this — the source taxonomy has no acceptances line at all | Model as a linked pair (customer obligation Contract + acceptance liability Contract) with a netting rule on self-held. Flag the Part 2 gap back to the document owner |
| **Discount instruments** (T-bills, CP, BAs, some NCDs) | Bought at a discount, redeem at par. The Leg's rate treatment is fixed-or-floating with a day count; there is no discount-basis quotation and **no stated owner for accretion / effective interest**. D2 §6 publishes "accruals, amortisation schedules" to D7, implying D2 computes EIR — but D2 §1 says it never computes fair value and §4.1 never mentions accretion | Add a `zero/discount` rate treatment with quotation basis (discount rate vs yield), and settle EIR ownership explicitly. EIR needs fees and expected life, which is a D7 judgment on a D2 schedule — say so |
| **Compounded-in-arrears RFRs** | §4.1 step 3 states a rule it calls precise: "a past reset is a stored fact; a future reset is a market data query." For a compounded-in-arrears RFR with lookback or observation shift, the **current period is partly observed** — some daily fixings exist, some do not. There is a third case, and it is now the market standard for the entire IR complex | Add the partially-fixed period as an explicit case in the pipeline, with the observation-shift convention on the Leg. Also add the RFR conventions (lookback, lockout, observation period shift, payment delay) to the Leg convention set |
| **MTM-resetting cross-currency swaps** | Now the market standard for CCS. One leg's notional resets periodically to the prevailing FX rate. Pipeline step 2 (notional resolution) handles amortisation profiles and inflation indexation only — this notional is a function of *future FX fixings* | Add `market-resetting` as a notional resolution type |
| **Futures** | See C4(d). No contractual cashflows; contributes to gap via notional and to risk via delta. Also CCP-cleared (no bilateral counterparty), initial margin, and cheapest-to-deliver optionality on bond futures | Requires the sensitivity intermediate (§3.2 below) plus a CCP counterparty type in D1 |
| **CDS** | The credit-event leg is contingent on a **credit event, which is not an exercise right** — Optionality (§2.3) models call/put/cancel/extend/conversion/trigger with an exercise party. A credit event has no exercise party. Also missing: upfront + standard running coupon (100/500bp), accrued-on-default, and auction settlement | Either widen Optionality to cover non-elective triggers with a reference entity and recovery assumption, or add a `credit_event` contingency descriptor. The former is probably cleaner |
| **Equity-classified AT1** | Part 2 C.5 explicitly contemplates equity-classified AT1. The accounting classification dimension is FVTPL / FVOCI / amortised cost — **there is no "equity" value**, so hard rule 1 blocks booking it. And its coupons are **discretionary distributions from retained earnings**, not contractual interest — another break of the cashflow premise, and it still needs 100% ASF in NSFR and CET1/AT1 treatment in D13 | Add `equity` to the accounting classification enum and state the discretionary-coupon projection rule |
| **Manufactured payments** | Sec lending and repo pass coupons and dividends on the collateral through to the lender. The Cashflow `Type` enum (principal, interest, fee, premium, principal exchange, margin) has no value for this, and it is a real cash movement | Add `manufactured payment` to the type enum |
| **Central bank facilities** | Three specifics the model does not reach: (a) **conditional-rate facilities** (TLTRO-style, where the rate depends on meeting a lending benchmark) are neither fixed nor floating-index, and their below-market element raises an IFRS 9 vs IAS 20 question; (b) **marginal lending / discount window drawings are created from collateral pool state**, i.e. D6 originates a Contract, inverting the D4 → D2 flow; (c) repo tenders are bid and **partially allotted**, which is a D4 pre-booking state | Add a `conditional` rate treatment or model the benefit as a separate contract; add D6 as a contract-originating source in §6; state the allotment lifecycle |
| **Facilities received vs granted** | Undrawn commitments are modelled with drawdown models — right for facilities the bank *grants*. For committed lines the bank *receives* from other banks (Part 1 §8), the LCR rule is that they provide **no inflow credit at all**. Direction is not distinguished | Make `direction` on contingent contracts drive the regulatory rule, and state the zero-inflow rule explicitly — it is a classic modelling error that inflates LCR |
| **Physical commodity / gold holdings** | Part 1 §7 names gold. A bullion holding has no maturity, no cashflow and no Part 2 line | Covered by the Balance object (C1), plus the commodity leg quantity variant (C4) |
| **Notice deposits** | Contractual maturity is "notice period from the day notice is given" — indeterminate until notice. Treated as an NMD-adjacent case with no stated rule | State the convention (typically: contractual = notice period from reporting date; behavioural = modelled) |

### 3.2 The "everything is cashflows" premise — where it actually breaks

The premise is right for **liquidity, accrual, settlement and gap on linear instruments**. It is
wrong as a universal, and the artifacts over-claim in one specific sentence: parent §2's "liquidity
ladder, **repricing gap**, NII, accrual and settlement are all aggregations of cashflows."

Repricing gap is not, for three classes:

- **Options** (caps, floors, swaptions, callable bonds, CoCos) contribute a **delta-equivalent**, not
a cashflow. D2 §4.2 says the Cashflow's rate treatment "drives repricing gap" — for a swaption
that produces a nonsense bucket.
- **Futures** contribute notional exposure with no cashflows at all.
- **Equity, commodity and non-contractual balance sheet** contribute to EVE and NSFR with no
cashflows and no repricing basis.

EVE is safe because it is a present value and routes through D8. NII on a behavioural basis is safe.
Gap is the exposed one.

**Fix.** Restate the premise precisely, in both artifacts: *"Cashflow is the universal intermediate*
*for liquidity, accrual and settlement, and for repricing gap on linear instruments. Non-linear*
*positions and non-contractual balances require a parallel **exposure** intermediate — notional or*
*delta-equivalent by repricing bucket — supplied by D8 alongside the valuation."* Then add
`exposure_by_bucket` to D8's published contract, next to value, cashflows and sensitivities. This is
a one-line addition to D8's interface and it closes the gap properly rather than papering it.

### 3.3 IFRS 9 and hedge accounting

**The §2.6 bifurcation claim is correct.** Verified: IFRS 9 4.3.2 requires a hybrid with a host that
is a financial asset in scope to be classified in its entirety (no separation); 4.3.3 retains
separation for financial liability and non-financial hosts. The two-independent-triggers reasoning
built on it is sound and is one of the better pieces of thinking in the artifact. Three corrections
around it:

| Issue | Detail | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The liability trigger is over-stated** | The table says structured deposits issued → "bifurcation applies unless FVO-designated". Bifurcation applies only if the embedded derivative is **not closely related**. An interest-rate cap or floor embedded in a deposit that is **at or out of the money at issuance and unleveraged is closely related** and is *not* separated (IFRS 9 B4.3.8(b)); plain inflation linkage is closely related, leveraged is not; an issuer call at approximately amortised cost is closely related. Part 1 §8 says "structured deposits (wholesale, **index/rate**-linked)" — so the rate-linked half of that population is likely not bifurcated at all | Restate the Tier 2 liability trigger as: *not closely related* **and** *would meet the derivative definition standalone* **and** *the hybrid is not measured at FVTPL*. As written, the rule over-tiers a whole product class |
| **The FVO escape route is not free** | Designating a liability at fair value moves the **own-credit-risk component of fair value change to OCI** (IFRS 9 5.7.7). That creates a distinct OCI reserve line and a P&L/OCI attribution D7 must compute and D13 must handle in capital (own-credit gains are filtered out of CET1) | Note the consequence wherever FVO is offered as the alternative to bifurcation |
| **Macro / portfolio hedge accounting is entirely absent** | This is the dominant hedge accounting use case for a bank ALM platform and neither artifact mentions it. IFRS 9 does not address macro hedging; banks either apply the **IAS 39 portfolio fair value hedge of interest rate risk** (retained by the IFRS 9 7.2.21 policy choice, and the EU carve-out) or wait on the DRM project. A treasury platform that supports only one-to-one micro hedges cannot support the bank's actual banking-book hedging | Add portfolio/macro hedge accounting to D7's scope with an explicit statement of which framework is applied, and treat it as a Phase 4 requirement, not an extension |
| **A hedged item is often not a Contract** | §2.5 models the hedge relationship as a `CONTRACT_LINK` from hedging instrument to hedged item. But hedged items are routinely **a layer or proportion of a portfolio** ("the bottom €500m of the 5y fixed mortgage book"), a **forecast transaction** (cash flow hedges of forecast issuance), or a **risk component**. None of those is a Contract | Make the hedge relationship a first-class entity, not a contract link: hedging instrument(s) + hedged item *specification* (contract set, layer, proportion, forecast description, risk component) + designation date + effectiveness method + documentation reference |
| **Cost of hedging is missing** | The forward element of a forward and the foreign currency basis spread of a cross-currency swap may be excluded from the designation and deferred in OCI (IFRS 9 6.5.15–6.5.16). For a bank using CCS to hedge foreign-currency funding — which this one is, per Part 1 §8 — this is a material and permanent OCI line | Add to D7's scope and to the OCI reserve set behind Part 2 C.4 |
| **The effectiveness language is dated** | Parent §1 D7 says "effectiveness testing". IFRS 9 replaced the 80–125% bright line with a qualitative economic-relationship test, credit-risk-not-dominant, and a hedge ratio consistent with risk management, plus mandatory **rebalancing** and restricted voluntary discontinuation | Say "effectiveness assessment and rebalancing" and name the qualitative criteria. A vendor reading "effectiveness testing" will price an IAS 39 module |

### 3.4 Boundaries and interfaces

| Gap | Why it matters | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D2 ↔ D8 circular call for model-implied exercise** | §2.3 allows the exercise assumption to come from "D8's model-implied exercise" — but D8 needs D2's cashflows to run. For every callable bond and CoCo the call graph is circular with no stated resolution order | State a two-pass protocol: contractual projection → D8 exercise probabilities → assumption-set projection. Make the intermediate an explicit, versioned assumption artefact so it is reproducible |
| **Behavioural models: data or code?** | D2 "executes a behavioural model, it does not own or calibrate it" (§1). If models are code, every D9 recalibration is a D2 release and the boundary is fictional. If they are executable specifications, that is a significant unstated requirement | Specify the behavioural model interface contract: a declarative, versioned specification with a defined input vector (contract attributes, rate environment, seasoning, scenario) and a defined output (cashflow adjustment vector). Distinguish parameter change (data) from functional-form change (release) explicitly |
| **D5 is missing from D2's inbound table** | §5.1 requires cash and nostro position keeping at seconds granularity — that data comes from D5. Settlement and failure status also change when a cashflow is realised | Add D5 as an inbound source. Add a `settlement status` to the Cashflow record, including an **overdue/failed** state — a cashflow due yesterday that did not settle must move in the ladder, and the `Certainty` enum has no value for it |
| **FTP at origination for core-banking-originated contracts** | §4.4 argument 3 — "an FTP rate is assigned at origination and locked for the contract's life" — is the strongest argument for contract-level storage. But retail loans originate in core banking, which does not know about FTP. Assignment at *ingestion* is not assignment at *origination*, and the rate will differ | Either specify a real-time FTP rate service that core banking calls at origination (a genuine integration requirement with a Phase-6 dependency), or state that the FTP rate is assigned at first ingestion using the origination-date curve, and accept the approximation openly. As written the argument assumes a capability that does not exist |
| **Quarantine policy breaks the balance sheet** | §2.4 rule 1 quarantines ingested records that fail classification. A quarantined retail deposit is *missing* from the balance sheet — which does not make ratios "quietly wrong", it makes the balance sheet not balance, and the daily batch will produce quarantines from day one | Quarantine to a **reported suspense bucket** with a mandatory unclassified line in every report and a hard sign-off gate, not to exclusion. The point is visibility, and exclusion is less visible than a wrong classification |
| **Large exposures regime is unmentioned** | A separate Basel framework from RWA, with hard limits (25% of Tier 1), connected-counterparty grouping, and its own reporting. It spans D11's limits, D13's returns and D1's counterparty group hierarchy | One line in D13 and a limit type in the limit framework. D1 already carries group hierarchy, so the data is there |
| **"Basel IV"** | Not a formal standard — it is the market's name for the December 2017 Basel III finalisation. In a regulator-facing blueprint the imprecision reads as unfamiliarity | Say "Basel III, including the December 2017 finalisation (output floor, revised SA and IRB, FRTB, SA-CCR, CVA)". Naming FRTB also forces the trading/banking book boundary into the dimension set, which C2 needs anyway |

## 4. What holds up

Stated so the rework is targeted rather than wholesale.

- **The five-object spine** is correct, and Leg-as-collapse is the right insight. It needs one more rate treatment and one more object, not a redesign.
- **Contractual and behavioural as parallel, never override** (parent §2, D2 §4.3) is exactly right and is the single most common thing real systems get wrong. Keep the emphasis.
- **Bitemporality** (D2 §3) and cancel-and-correct are correct and non-negotiable, and the justification given is the right one.
- **D8's narrow contract** is the best boundary in the document and is what makes the buy decision in Phase 2 real. Protect it — the fix in §3.2 adds one field, it does not widen the interface.
- **The as-reported vs reproducible distinction** (§7.1) is a genuinely good piece of thinking that most designs miss, and it survives everything in C5.
- **Decision 5 (carry the entity field, build nothing)** is right, the cost-asymmetry argument is right, and the B.6 own-securitisation flag is a sharp catch. Add FCTR and NIH to the same list.
- **Decision 1's storage half** — contract-level storage for per-depositor run-off, concentration and FTP — is correct and well-argued. Only the *projection* half's rationale needs trimming (D7 above).
- **Liquidity before ALM** is the right sequencing call, for a reason adjacent to the one given (see C6).
- **Phase 4 named as where these programmes die** is the most valuable sentence in the blueprint and is correct.

## 5. Suggested order of remediation

1. **C2 + C1** — run the 40-line Part 2 map by hand and publish it. It will surface the Balance object and the missing dimensions concretely, and it is the cheapest possible validation of the whole model. Half a day.
2. **C3** — re-cut the phase boundaries around the classification engine, the limit framework and the audit/four-eyes controls. This changes the programme plan, so it should happen before anyone commits to a sequence.
3. **C4 + §3.1** — add the third rate treatment, the externally-projected amortisation type, the quantity leg, and extend §2.2's worked decompositions to the untested instruments. Then re-test.
4. **C9 + C8** — add the Legal Agreement / netting set entity and the ECL interface. Both are small and both are blocking for later phases.
5. **C5** — fix the projection signature, version D1 reference data, add the digest and the reporting-date freeze.
6. **C7 + §3.3** — settle the D7 terms-payload boundary and correct the closely-related and macro-hedge treatment.
7. **C10** — add the two operational domains and give them phases.
8. **C6 + §2** — correct the LCR rationale and reconcile the contradictions. Mostly editorial once the above are settled.

Items 1–4 are worth doing before the blueprint is used to solicit a vendor quote. Items 5–8 are
worth doing before a build starts.
